UniFi Protect: Smart Security Cameras Without Monthly Cloud Fees

UniFi Protect is built around local recording and local AI, so homes and businesses can own their footage, choose their storage, and use secure remote access without paying a recurring license for every camera.

September 10, 2026 · Steve Smith

Monthly camera subscriptions have become normal enough that many homeowners and business owners assume they are unavoidable. Buy a camera, then keep paying every month for recording history, smart alerts, or access to the features that made the camera useful in the first place.

UniFi Protect takes a different approach. The cameras record to storage you control, the core video platform runs on your UniFi console or NVR, and Ubiquiti currently lists Protect camera licensing at $0. That makes it especially attractive for people searching for a security camera system without a monthly subscription.

For homes and businesses in Berthoud, Loveland, Fort Collins, and across Northern Colorado, the long-term value is not only about avoiding a bill. It is about owning the system and deciding how your recordings are handled.

The important difference: your recordings are local

Ubiquiti's current Protect data-security documentation states that video recordings and AI events are stored locally on the customer's UniFi OS Console, such as an NVR or compatible gateway. Ubiquiti also says there is no cloud backup of your video by default.

That means your recorder and storage are part of the design. You decide how much storage to install, who can access the system, and how long recordings should be retained.

This is one of the reasons we consider UniFi a strong fit for customers who want something more substantial than consumer WiFi cameras. Our earlier article, Why UniFi Cameras Are One of the Best Security Systems for Homes and Businesses, explains the broader platform advantages.

$0 camera licensing is different from “nothing can ever cost extra”

Ubiquiti currently advertises no licensing fees for UniFi Protect cameras, including deployments ranging from a few cameras to very large systems. Core recording and AI processing run on the local Protect system rather than requiring a paid per-camera cloud license.

That does not mean every possible storage destination or third-party service is free. Protect supports optional off-site archiving to services such as Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, and NAS storage. Those destinations may have their own storage costs, subscriptions, or capacity limits.

The practical distinction is that optional archiving is not the same thing as making cloud storage mandatory just to keep normal camera recordings.

Remote access still works

Local recording does not mean you have to be sitting in front of the NVR. UniFi provides remote viewing through Site Manager and the Protect mobile app. According to Ubiquiti, the remote session connects securely to the customer's UniFi console while the recordings remain stored locally.

For a homeowner, that can mean checking the driveway or front door while traveling. For a business owner, it can mean reviewing a loading dock, parking lot, office entrance, or warehouse from another location. Remote management is currently offered as an optional free feature.

The AI processing is local too

Protect's privacy model goes beyond recording location. Ubiquiti says Protect detection, recognition, and AI-enhanced video processing happens on the local network rather than sending surveillance video to the cloud for analysis.

Depending on the camera model and equipment in the system, Protect can identify useful events such as people and vehicles, while supported G6 and AI-series cameras can add capabilities such as face recognition and license plate recognition. Smart Detection Zones let you define which parts of the image should generate events.

This matters because a camera system is much easier to use when you can find the event you want instead of scrolling through hours of motion recordings.

You control how and when cameras record

UniFi Protect currently supports continuous recording, detection-only recording, and recording schedules. Retention can be managed automatically or configured around a desired period.

A residential system might prioritize several weeks of continuous coverage around entrances. A business may want longer retention on critical areas while using detection-only recording in lower-activity spaces. The right answer depends on camera resolution, camera count, activity, storage capacity, and how important older footage is to the customer.

Storage can scale with the system

One advantage of local recording is that retention becomes a design choice instead of a cloud subscription tier. Smaller deployments can use compatible UniFi consoles with onboard or attached storage, while larger systems can use dedicated UNVR-class recorders and multiple drives.

For larger commercial installations, storage planning should happen before cameras are purchased. The goal is not simply to buy the biggest drives possible. We size around the number and resolution of cameras, recording mode, expected activity, redundancy needs, and target retention period.

Privacy is a major part of the value

Ubiquiti states that it cannot view, store, or access customers' Protect video streams during remote access. The company also documents end-to-end encrypted remote sessions, and supported cameras can use stream encryption between the cameras and the UniFi console.

No surveillance platform should be treated as magically secure, and permissions still need to be configured correctly. But local recording reduces one common privacy concern: your everyday surveillance archive does not have to live in a vendor's cloud account.

Why subscription-free becomes more valuable as camera count grows

A recurring charge on one doorbell may not seem significant. The economics change when a property needs eight, twelve, twenty, or more cameras.

Businesses in particular may need entrances, exits, parking areas, cash-handling locations, loading docks, warehouses, server rooms, gates, and exterior perimeters covered. With a per-camera subscription platform, recurring fees can increase every time another camera is added.

With Protect, adding cameras affects hardware, network capacity, PoE power, recorder performance, and storage, but Ubiquiti's current Protect model does not add a recurring camera-license fee.

It works best as part of a professionally designed network

Most UniFi security deployments are not only camera projects. They are network projects too. Cameras need reliable cabling, sufficient PoE power, appropriate switching, recorder bandwidth, secure network segmentation, and battery backup.

That is where UniFi becomes especially useful. Protect can live alongside UniFi Network and UniFi Access, making it possible to manage WiFi, switching, cameras, door access, and related infrastructure within the same ecosystem.

How UniFi Protect compares with consumer cloud camera systems

Consumer camera systems are not automatically bad. Ring, Arlo, and similar platforms can be an easy fit for someone who wants a few wireless cameras and does not want to maintain local recording hardware.

Protect starts to make more sense when the priorities shift toward wired reliability, local ownership, longer-term scalability, centralized management, and lower recurring software costs.

Good camera placement matters more than the logo on the camera

Even a very capable camera will disappoint if it is mounted too high, pointed into glare, placed too far from the identification area, or expected to capture a license plate from an unrealistic angle.

Before choosing cameras, we look at what each location needs to accomplish: general awareness, facial identification, license plates, doorway coverage, wide parking-lot views, or monitoring a specific asset. Our camera placement guide covers the basic planning principles.
