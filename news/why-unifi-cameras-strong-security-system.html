UniFi Protect cameras

Why UniFi Cameras Are One of the Best Security Systems for Homes and Businesses

For people who want professional wired cameras, local video storage, useful smart detections, and one system they can grow over time, UniFi Protect is one of the strongest options we install and support.

September 8, 2026 · Steve Smith

There is no single security camera system that is right for every property. Some people need a simple doorbell. Others need a few cameras around a home. A business may need cameras across offices, parking areas, warehouses, gates, and multiple buildings.

That is why we like UniFi Protect. It is not just a collection of cameras. It is a complete surveillance platform that can combine cameras, local recording, smart detections, network infrastructure, door access, remote viewing, and long-term expansion under one interface.

For homes and businesses in Berthoud, Loveland, Fort Collins, and across Northern Colorado, that combination makes UniFi Protect a very strong security choice when the goal is reliability and ownership rather than another collection of disconnected cloud devices.

Your recordings stay local

One of the biggest differences between UniFi Protect and many consumer camera platforms is where the footage lives. According to Ubiquiti's current Protect data-security documentation, video recordings and AI events are stored locally on the customer's UniFi console or NVR. Ubiquiti also states that it does not view or store the video streams used for remote access.

That gives the property owner control over storage capacity, retention, access policies, and how long recordings are kept. For many homeowners and businesses, that is a meaningful advantage over systems built primarily around cloud storage.

Smart detections make footage easier to use

A security system is only useful if you can find the event you care about. Modern UniFi cameras support more than basic motion alerts. Ubiquiti's current feature matrix includes model-dependent detection of people, vehicles, animals, speech and other audio events, along with capabilities such as facial recognition and license plate recognition on supported cameras or with supported AI accessories.

That means you can search and review meaningful events instead of scrubbing through hours of video every time something happens. Protect also supports motion zones, smart-detection zones, and privacy zones, which helps reduce useless alerts and keep cameras focused on the parts of the property that matter.

The exact AI features vary by camera model, so camera selection matters. We design around the view and the result you need instead of simply installing the same camera everywhere.

PoE cameras are built for dependable installations

Many UniFi cameras use Power over Ethernet. A single network cable carries both power and data back to the network switch. For a professionally installed system, that is usually preferable to relying on WiFi signal quality and individual wall adapters at every camera location.

A wired PoE design also lets us centralize the cameras, switches, recorder, and battery backup. If the system is properly planned, critical network and camera equipment can continue operating through brief utility interruptions even if the rest of the property loses power.

For new construction or remodels, camera locations are worth planning before drywall. Our camera placement guide explains why mounting height, field of view, lighting, and identification distance matter as much as camera resolution.

You can choose recording mode and retention

UniFi Protect currently supports continuous recording, detection-only recording, and configurable recording schedules. Retention can also be adjusted around the needs of the property. A small home and a commercial building do not need the same storage design.

The number of cameras, resolution, frame rate, activity level, recording mode, and desired number of retained days all affect recorder sizing. If you are budgeting a project, our guide to security camera installation cost in Northern Colorado explains the major cost drivers beyond the cameras themselves.

One platform can cover a small home or a much larger property

UniFi's camera lineup spans compact fixed cameras, higher-resolution cameras, doorbells, specialty AI cameras, license-plate cameras, and PTZ models. That gives us flexibility to design around the job instead of forcing one camera type into every location.

A home might need coverage at the driveway, front entry, backyard, and side gates. A business might need wide parking-lot coverage, entrance identification, loading dock cameras, interior views, and specialty coverage at a gate. A rural Northern Colorado property may also need cameras at a barn, detached shop, driveway entrance, or another building connected with fiber or a point-to-point link.

Protect works especially well when the network is designed with it

This is an area where UniFi has a major practical advantage. The cameras can be part of the same managed infrastructure as the property's switches, WiFi, routing, and other UniFi systems.

For us, that means we can look at the property as one connected system. Camera bandwidth, PoE budgets, switch capacity, VLANs, recorder placement, storage, UPS runtime, and remote-building connectivity can all be planned together.

That is different from adding cameras after the fact and hoping the existing network can handle them. You can learn more about our UniFi Protect security systems and our broader security camera installation services.

No mandatory per-camera cloud recording subscription for local Protect recording

UniFi Protect's core recording model is local. You buy the cameras, provide the recorder or compatible console and storage, and the system records locally without requiring a typical per-camera cloud recording plan for that core functionality.

Optional services and future product offerings can change, so we do not recommend choosing a platform based on one pricing claim alone. The more important difference is architectural: Protect is designed around local ownership of surveillance footage rather than making cloud storage the required center of the system.

If recurring cloud-camera fees are part of what you are comparing, see our UniFi Protect vs. Ring, UniFi Protect vs. Arlo, and UniFi Protect vs. consumer camera systems guides.

Remote access without turning the system into a cloud recorder

Local recording does not mean you have to be on the property to use the system. Protect supports secure remote viewing through the UniFi interface. Ubiquiti says the remote session is established through an encrypted connection to the customer's UniFi console while the video itself remains on the customer's system.

For a homeowner, that means checking cameras while traveling. For a business owner, it can mean reviewing an event without driving to the building. For multi-site environments, it gives administrators a consistent interface without changing the local-recording model.

Where UniFi Protect makes the most sense

We tend to recommend UniFi Protect when the customer wants a professionally wired system, local storage, good remote access, smart detections, and the ability to grow without replacing the entire platform later.

  • Homes that need more than a basic doorbell camera.
  • Custom homes where camera cabling can be planned before drywall.
  • Small businesses that want cameras integrated with a managed network.
  • Offices, retail spaces, warehouses, churches, and professional buildings.
  • Rural properties with barns, shops, gates, and detached buildings.
  • Properties that want to combine cameras with UniFi Access door or gate control.

Good cameras still need a good design

The platform is only part of the solution. Camera placement, lens selection, mounting height, night lighting, storage, cable pathways, network design, and power protection determine whether the footage will actually be useful when you need it.

A 4K camera pointed at the wrong angle can still give you a poor image. A license-plate camera mounted too far away or at the wrong angle can miss plates. A PTZ camera cannot replace properly placed fixed cameras everywhere. And a recorder without enough storage can shorten retention below what the owner expected.

That is why Berthoud WiFi designs the system around the property first and chooses the UniFi equipment second.
